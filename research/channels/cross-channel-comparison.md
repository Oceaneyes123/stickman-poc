## 1. Patterns common to every channel with real reach

1. **One idea per scene.** No sampled frame carries two competing messages.
2. **A repeating device the viewer learns.** Planet label, day card, chapter card,
   dot grid, coloured character. The device is the channel's signature and it costs
   nothing per video after the first.
3. **Text as annotation, never as transcript.** No channel burns the narration onto
   the screen.
4. **A scale or comparison in the first 25 seconds.** A dot next to a billion
   dollars; a person next to a Sun; a flat next to a city.
5. **A visual answer to the spoken claim at the same moment.** Every sampled beat has
   the graphic doing the sentence's work, not decorating it.

## 2. Techniques unique to one channel (and whether they travel)

| Technique | Channel | Travels? |
|---|---|---|
| Persistent section label naming the current object | Paint Explainer | **Yes** — cheapest retention device in the corpus |
| Depleting icon row as a clock | Beanie Survives | **Yes**, with the caveat that ours must be proportional or explicitly symbolic |
| Borrowed artefacts (news, chart screenshots, interviews) | How Money Works | **No** — licensing and authenticity cost |
| Face-free characters carrying emotion by staging | Alan Becker | Partly — needs choreography skill |
| Per-shot bespoke illustration | Kurzgesagt | **No** — studio cost |
| Definition card on a flat colour field | James Jani | **Yes** |
| Two-pose comedy across a prop | TheOdd1sOut | **Yes**, for comic relief beats only |

## 3. Techniques that should not be combined

- **Paint Explainer's flat white + How Money Works' live-action b-roll.** The white
  field exists so the diagram is the only object; dropping footage into it destroys
  the rule.
- **Alan Becker's face-free stick figures + narrated finance.** A character with no
  face cannot react to a bill; the audience needs the face for the story to land.
- **Beanie's second-person "you" + a narrator who also says "I".** Beanie never
  breaks into first person; the whole format depends on the viewer being the subject.
- **Kurzgesagt's tonal inversions + a fixed brand palette.** Inversions work because
  each video owns its palette; a locked brand palette would flatten them.
- **Odd1sOut's 240 wpm + Paint's 8-second diagrams.** Fast speech over slow diagrams
  reads as two unrelated tracks.

## 4. High-impact, code-producible (ranked by value ÷ cost)

1. **Section label in a fixed position**, driven by a scene prop.
2. **One measurement annotation per diagram**, coloured like the element it labels.
3. **One stand-in figure per beat**, scaled against the environment (tiny vs huge is
   the whole trick).
4. **Reusable set relit** for time passage, instead of a new background.
5. **Definition card** for the one or two equations a finance script needs.
6. **State card with a depleting row** when the story tracks an amount over time.

## 5. High-impact but costly (do not attempt yet)

- Bespoke illustration per beat (Kurzgesagt).
- Licensed footage and expert interviews (How Money Works).
- Choreographed group action (Alan Becker).
- Live-action dramatisation (James Jani).

## 6. One coherent identity, not a collage

The evidence supports exactly one combination for a 10–15 minute finance explainer in
this repo: **The Paint Explainer's diagram grammar + James Jani's definition cards +
Beanie's state card, with the repo's existing rig and palette.** Everything else in
the corpus either needs assets this pipeline does not have or would fight the brand's
calm register. That is a narrower identity than the brief's ambition, and it is the
only one the evidence actually supports.
