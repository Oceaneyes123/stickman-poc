# Storyboard patterns — narration to visual

Maps real caption text at sampled timestamps to the frame sampled at that timestamp
(`../evidence/frames/`). Narration quotes are verbatim from captions, including
recognition errors. Where a timestamp has no nearby caption (silence, music, or no
speech at all) the cell says so rather than being filled in.

## 1. The Paint Explainer — planet sections as a repeatable unit

Structure: one planet = label + one hostile mechanism + one measurement + one
stand-in figure. The section boundary is the planet name spoken aloud and printed on
screen at the same moment.

| Time | Narration | Visual (observed) | Animation implied | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 s | "Mercury / Mercury is the closest planet to the sun, but if you think that means it's hot all the…" | Wide, white field: huge Sun left, tiny burning stick figure right; "MERCURY" centred top | Figure engulfed in flame shapes | Cold open: name the place, show a person dying in it |
| 20 s | "This extreme imbalance happens because Mercury doesn't have a proper atmosphere to regulate temperature." | Medium: textured Mercury with pale halo, red arrows circling, thermometer left, "atmosphere" in red | Arrows radiate around the planet | Mechanism as a diagram, not a sentence |
| 50 s | "It would make slowing down a spaceship much harder, increasing the chances of a high speed crash." | Wide: Sun upper-left, lander upper-right with orange exhaust, grey horizon lower third | Descent path | Consequence of the mechanism |
| 90 s | "Venus. / Venus is the closest planet to Earth, sitting just 40 million kilometers away." | Wide diagram: small Venus and Earth joined by a red dashed line, both labelled | Dashed distance line | Section transition carried by a comparison, not a title |
| 150 s | "But before long, the pressure, about 92 bars compared to Earth's one bar at sea level, would crush your suit." | Medium: astronaut centred on tan ground, red "pressure" arrows above, "about 92 bar" and "1 bar" labels | Downward arrows | The measurement is the beat |
| 260 s | "you'd reach the deepest point ever explored. This is how far NASA's Galileo probe got back in 1995." | Wide: blue gradient, Galileo Probe descending diagonally, "deepest point" label | Descent | Depth as literal distance |
| 380 s | "would transition into a liquid state. If you somehow survived long enough" | Medium close: ringed Saturn centred, red dashed line to centre labelled "center" | Cutaway line | Cross-section as the last beat of a section |
| 470 s | "but the inner layers are intensely hot due to immense pressure and heat from its core." | Medium: Neptune cutaway, blue shell, hot core, red inward arrows, thermometer | Cutaway reveal | Section close: hostile interior |

**Grammar to copy:** name → mechanism → measurement → consequence, with the same
label position and the same red-arrow vocabulary every time. Ten sections of this in
one video is why the channel can ship weekly.

## 2. Beanie Survives — set + counter + delta

| Time | Narration | Visual | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 s | "You live on the 30th floor. Until tonight, that was the worst thing about your flat." | Wide living room at night, character small against skyline windows, warm lamp | Establish the box and the height |
| 20 s | "Some of them are not running away. Your neighbor is in the hallway, knocking on every door." | Fisheye peephole: corridor, worried face, bandaged forearm | Threat without showing a zombie |
| 45 s | "one person needs a gallon a day. You have just bought yourself six weeks." | Full-screen card: "WATER / Stored 45 gal", nine jug icons, "1 jug = 5 gallons = 5 days" | Convert stock into time — the show's core trick |
| 80 s | "The power dies at noon. The taps cough and stop." | Same living room as 5 s, relit cold grey, candle | Time passing on the same set |
| 140 s | "It rains. You put every bowl you own on the window ledge." | Card: "SUPPLIES · DAY 9", water 35 gal with faded jugs, "Food, rationed 16 days", "+3 gallons of rain" | Delta since last card |
| 250 s | "From today, everything runs out twice as fast. You are sick of cold beans." | Kitchen, cool blue-grey, character full body | New set for a new problem |
| 350 s | "First, the hallway. Rule one said noise is a dinner bell." | Same kitchen, tighter, darker, candlelit | Same set, escalated stakes |
| 480 s | "sitting on the hatch with everything heavy you could find. Two days of food, a city of them below." | Card: "SUPPLIES · DAY 30", water 5,000 gal, "Food, half rations 2 days", "A city of them below." | Payoff: one resource solved, the other gone |

**Grammar to copy:** second person, a physical box, and a card that returns with
worse numbers. The card is the story's clock.

## 3. How Money Works — borrowed evidence plus staged metaphor

| Time | Narration | Visual | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 s | "fewer young people than ever are going to be able to buy a house in their lifetime and at the same time homes are getting more expensive every year" | Live-action b-roll + Fortune headline card | Establish topicality with a real artefact |
| 20 s | "down payment uh is almost unimaginable yeah rent gets raised you know a thousand percent with no notice…" | Live-action presenter b-roll in a grocery store | Human voice, real place |
| 45 s | "future you already know this but if you're in the 75% of my audience that doesn't own a home it's probably because you can't afford one" | Wojak meme with hoodie text, flat green grid | Direct address through a joke |
| 75 s | "end game here if people can't afford a home then house prices can't go up anymore right wrong" | Staged family scene, RIGHT/WRONG buttons, FOR SALE sign | Turn the thesis into a visible choice |
| 150 s | "because the price of the average house grew just as fast as their savings the average home earns as much as the average person" | Balance scale: man on one pan, house on the other | The comparison the sentence makes |
| 300 s | "million homes were sold in 2021 but now only 4 million homes are selling at an annualized rate" | Trading Economics chart screenshot | The number, unmodified |
| 480 s | "third group are investors investors only care about the return on their assets" | CBC expert interview, lower-third | Authority for the claim |
| 660 s | "the bonus fourth reason is that wealthy families are just doing a better job of holding on to more properties" | "REASON #4" card over blurred plate | Chapter marker |

**What transfers:** 75 s and 150 s — turning a sentence into a physical comparison.
**What does not:** the news card, the chart screenshot and the interview, which need
licensed footage this pipeline will never have.

## 5. Alan Becker — no narration at all

The sampled episode has no captions and no speech; the audio track is music and
effects. Story is carried entirely by staging:

| Time | Beat | Visual | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 s | Group walks in | Wide, five coloured figures in a line, orange holding a map | Introduce cast and goal |
| 25 s | They reach an edge | Wide, group at a ledge, empty space below | Anticipation staged in composition |
| 60 s | The drop | Very wide multi-level mineshaft, diagonal spider swarm | Scale reveal |
| 100 s | Speed | Frame filled by a diagonal rail | Motion carried by geometry |
| 180 s | Reaction | Close group, overlapping heads, arms raised | Emotion without faces |
| 280–360 s | Chase | Medium-wide action, then wide running line | Sustained action |
| 445 s | End | Credits over dimmed cave | — |

**Lesson:** a stick figure needs no face and no voice if the staging changes scale
and direction. **Warning:** this format cannot carry a finance explanation.

## 6. Scene-type vocabulary (normalised across the corpus)

Every sampled beat falls into one of nine scene types. The count is how many of the
six sampled videos used it at a sampled timestamp.

| Scene type | Used by | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Diagram with label | Paint, HMW (chart) | One object, one arrow, one measurement |
| Character in environment | Beanie, Odd1sOut, Alan | A set, a figure, contact shadow |
| State card | Beanie | Text block + icon row |
| Chapter label | Paint (planet name), HMW ("REASON #4") | One text element, fixed position |
| Visual metaphor | HMW (scale, RIGHT/WRONG) | Two objects that can be compared |
| Borrowed artefact | HMW (news, chart, interview) | **Not reproducible** |
| Wide establishing | Kurzgesagt, Beanie, Alan | Layered background, small figure |
| Extreme close-up | Kurzgesagt | One feature filling frame |
| Typography card | James Jani, HMW | Flat field + one sentence |

Six of the nine are reproducible in a code-driven pipeline today; two (borrowed
artefact, extreme close-up of a rendered face) are not, and one (metaphor) needs
original prop design per video.
