Sam bought a jacket he wasn't planning to buy.

He'd been looking at it for almost a week. A nice jacket, nothing special. The kind you see online and think, "That looks good."

But it was two hundred and forty dollars.

Every time Sam opened the page, he looked at the price, thought about it for a moment, and closed the tab.

He liked it. He just didn't want to spend that much on a jacket.

Then, on Friday, he noticed something at checkout.

**Pay in four. Sixty dollars today.**

Sixty dollars sounded a lot better.

He'd just been paid. He had sixty dollars.

So he placed the order.

A few days later, the jacket arrived.

He tried it on, looked in the mirror, and was pretty happy with it.

The strange part was that he probably wouldn't have bought it if he'd needed to pay the full price that day.

But sixty dollars?

That felt fine.

And that was how Sam started using Buy Now, Pay Later.

---

A week later, Sam was looking for new headphones.

His old ones still worked, although one side had started cutting out whenever he moved the cable.

He found a pair he liked.

A little expensive, but there was that option again.

Four smaller payments.

He remembered the jacket. That purchase had been easy.

Why not?

He bought the headphones.

Then came a pair of shoes.

He'd seen someone wearing them at work and decided to look them up.

He wasn't really shopping for shoes. He was just looking.

But they were on sale, and once again, he didn't have to pay the full price right away.

So he bought them too.

A few days later, his friends invited him to a concert.

The tickets were more expensive than he'd expected.

Normally, he would've told them he'd think about it.

But the ticket website offered installments.

That made the decision easier.

He bought the ticket.

And just like that, Sam had a new jacket, new headphones, new shoes, and something to look forward to next month.

He hadn't made any huge purchases.

At least, that's how it felt.

Every time he bought something, he only needed enough money for the first payment.

And he always had enough for that.

---

The payments started showing up a few days later.

First, a notification about the jacket.

Then the headphones.

Then another one for the shoes.

Each message was pretty ordinary.

A payment was coming up. Make sure there was enough money in the account.

Nothing alarming.

Sam glanced at the notifications and carried on with his day.

After all, he'd agreed to those payments.

He knew they were coming.

What he hadn't really thought about was how close together they'd arrive.

One payment on Tuesday.

Another on Thursday.

Two more the following week.

And then the next round started.

Sam had checked whether he could afford each purchase when he made it.

But he hadn't looked at what all those purchases would mean together.

That's the part that caught him off guard.

When you split a purchase into installments, the full price doesn't disappear.

You just pay it over time.

That can be useful. Sometimes you'd rather spread out a planned expense instead of paying everything at once.

But for Sam, something else was happening.

He was buying things he normally would've waited to buy.

A jacket he didn't really need.

Headphones he could've put off replacing.

Shoes he'd only started thinking about because someone at work had a pair.

He wasn't buying all these things because they suddenly became cheaper.

He was buying them because the first payment looked easy.

And as long as that first payment was small enough, saying yes didn't feel like a big decision.

---

At first, none of this caused a problem.

Sam went to work.

He paid his rent.

He bought groceries.

He went out with friends.

Everything felt normal.

His new jacket was hanging beside the door.

The headphones were on his desk.

The shoes were still in their box because he hadn't found a reason to wear them yet.

He was enjoying the things he'd bought.

And every few days, another payment came out of his account.

He didn't pay much attention to it.

Until one Monday morning.

Sam was already running late for work.

He got into his car, turned the key, and heard a clicking sound.

He tried again.

Nothing.

The battery was dead.

Not exactly how he wanted to start the week.

He called a mechanic, arranged to have the battery replaced, and found out what it would cost.

It wasn't an enormous expense.

But it wasn't something he'd planned for either.

He opened his banking app.

His paycheck had arrived a few days earlier.

Rent was paid. Bills were covered.

But several installment payments were still due before his next payday.

Suddenly, the money in his account didn't feel quite as available as it had the week before.

He could afford the car repair.

That wasn't the problem.

He just couldn't pay for it as comfortably as he'd expected.

So he transferred some money from his savings.

Paid for the repair.

And went to work.

Problem solved.

Except now his savings were lower than he'd wanted.

And there were still payments coming.

---

That evening, Sam sat on the couch and checked his banking app.

He wasn't in debt trouble.

He hadn't missed a payment.

He hadn't even paid any extra fees.

But his account was looking a little thinner than usual.

He opened the payment reminders and scrolled through them.

The jacket.

The headphones.

The shoes.

The concert tickets.

He'd forgotten how many things he'd bought in such a short time.

Not forgotten the purchases themselves.

He remembered all of those.

What he'd lost track of was how many payments were still waiting.

He opened his calendar and started marking the dates.

Tuesday. Friday. Next Monday. The following Thursday.

There were more of them than he expected.

And now, every time he looked at his account, he had to remember that some of that money was already needed for something else.

It was a strange feeling.

He had a decent job. He'd been paid on time. He hadn't bought anything wildly expensive.

Yet he was suddenly being careful about buying lunch.

A month earlier, he wouldn't have thought twice about it.

Now he was checking his banking app before ordering.

---

The following day, Sam was having lunch with Alex.

Alex noticed Sam looking at his phone.

"Everything okay?"

"Yeah. Just checking my account."

"Again?"

Sam laughed.

"I've got all these payments coming out. I keep forgetting about them."

"What payments?"

Sam told him about the jacket, the headphones, the shoes, and the concert tickets.

Alex looked at him.

"You bought all of that this month?"

"Yeah, but I didn't pay for everything at once."

"I figured."

Sam shrugged.

"Each payment was pretty small."

Alex nodded.

"Yeah. That's what makes it tempting."

Sam showed him the jacket on his phone.

"Look. It wasn't even that expensive when I split it up."

Alex looked at the picture.

"Would you have bought it if you had to pay the whole amount that day?"

Sam stopped for a moment.

"Probably not."

"Why?"

"Because I didn't want to spend that much on a jacket."

Alex handed the phone back.

"Well, that's still how much the jacket costs."

Sam knew that.

Of course he knew that.

The price had been right there on the checkout page.

But he'd been thinking about sixty dollars when he bought it.

Not the whole amount.

And he'd done the same thing with almost everything else.

---

Alex wasn't against installment payments.

He'd used them before.

A while back, he'd bought a laptop for work and spread the cost over several payments.

He'd already planned to buy the laptop, knew what it would cost, and had room for the payments.

Splitting the bill was just more convenient.

But he didn't use installments to decide whether he wanted to buy something in the first place.

That was the difference.

Sam saw a smaller payment and started thinking that something was affordable.

Alex decided whether the purchase was worth the full price before thinking about how to pay for it.

It sounded obvious when Alex explained it.

But Sam realized he'd been doing it the other way around.

He'd see something he liked.

Decide it was too expensive.

Then see the installment option and change his mind.

The jacket was the perfect example.

He'd already closed that tab twice.

He'd basically decided not to buy it.

Then the payment option showed up, and suddenly he was happy to order it.

Nothing about the jacket had changed.

He just didn't have to pay for all of it that day.

---

That night, Sam looked through his purchases again.

He wasn't going to return everything.

He actually liked the jacket.

The headphones were good.

And he was looking forward to the concert.

But the shoes?

He still hadn't worn them.

He pulled the box out of his closet.

They looked nice.

He tried them on.

Walked around the room for a minute.

Then put them back in the box.

He checked whether he could still return them.

He could.

So he started the return.

It wasn't some dramatic change.

He didn't delete every shopping app or promise never to buy anything again.

He just realized that he'd been making purchases differently.

Before, when something felt too expensive, he usually waited.

Sometimes he'd come back to it later.

Sometimes he'd forget about it completely.

And sometimes, after a few days, he'd realize he didn't really want it.

With installments, he wasn't giving himself that time anymore.

The smaller payment made it easier to skip the waiting.

So he'd been buying things the moment he felt like buying them.

And now he had several weeks of payments to deal with.

---

Over the next month, Sam started paying closer attention.

He checked what payments were already scheduled before buying anything new.

He still used his card.

Still went out with friends.

Still bought things he liked.

But whenever he saw that familiar Pay in Four button, he looked at the full price first.

Sometimes he still thought the purchase was worth it.

Other times, he decided to wait.

One evening, he found a watch online.

He'd been looking for something similar for a while.

It was a nice watch.

A little expensive.

And right below the price was the same familiar offer.

Four easy payments.

He almost clicked it.

Then he remembered the jacket.

And the headphones.

And the shoes he'd returned.

He looked at the full price again.

Did he really want the watch that much?

Maybe.

But not today.

He closed the tab.

And went to make dinner.

---

Buy Now, Pay Later isn't automatically a bad thing.

If you understand the payments, can afford the full purchase, and know what's coming out of your account, it can be a convenient way to pay.

Some plans don't charge interest when you pay on time. Others may have interest or fees, depending on the terms.

The problem starts when the smaller payments make you buy things you wouldn't normally buy.

Because a small payment is still part of a bigger purchase
