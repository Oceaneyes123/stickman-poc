A two-hundred-forty-dollar jacket sitting on a store rack looks like a clear decision: either you spend two hundred forty dollars today, or you walk away.

At checkout, a second button changes what your attention lands on. Instead of the full tag, the screen highlights sixty dollars today.

Nothing about the jacket's price changed. What changed is that checkout turned one upfront decision into four separate sixty-dollar commitments spaced across the next six weeks.

Those remaining drafts quietly land on future dates on your calendar, often alongside other split purchases that felt just as small on the day you clicked.

When ordinary obligations arrive in the same month—rent, utilities, or an unexpected car service—those scheduled drafts already hold a claim on your paycheck.

A smaller first payment can make a purchase feel cheaper at checkout, even when the full commitment leaves less room later.
