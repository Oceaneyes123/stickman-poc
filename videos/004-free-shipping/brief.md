# Why Free Shipping Makes You Spend More

Research and script draft prepared 2026-11-17, GMT+8. Script-only handoff for The Money Trap.

## Editorial brief

- Viewer: An English-speaking working adult who has added something to an online order to avoid a delivery charge and wants to know whether that was actually a saving.
- Central question: How can removing a shipping charge make someone choose a more expensive order?
- Promise: Explain how a conditional free-shipping offer can turn an already-finished purchase into a search for additional products. Distinguish qualifying for an offer from getting good value overall.
- Story engine: Follow one explicitly imaginary shopping cart, examine the offer behind the decision, test the explanation against research, then return to the cart with a fair comparison.
- Human stake: A small checkout decision can use money the shopper had intended to keep available for something else. No debt, hardship or cumulative annual loss is invented.
- Core concept: A spending threshold can become a new shopping goal. Eliminating one fee does not necessarily minimize the full order price.
- Verified mechanism: Conditional shipping incentives affect basket formation and search. Zero-price experiments provide narrower supporting evidence about the appeal of free goods, not direct proof of shipping psychology. Responses vary and higher thresholds can backfire.
- Reasonable shopper motive: Avoid an unpopular extra charge and receive a useful product instead. A planned household purchase can make qualifying genuinely worthwhile.
- Retailer incentive: Encourage sufficient merchandise sales to help cover delivery costs. Increased sales are not the same as increased profit.
- End payoff: Compare the original delivered order with the enlarged delivered order, then decide whether the additional item is worth the additional outlay.
- Title scope: The title describes a documented possible effect, not an assertion that every free-shipping offer increases every shopper's spending.
- Packaging reference only: A clearly illustrative cart has a crossed-out delivery charge beside a higher order total. Optional label, "FREE DELIVERY. BIGGER ORDER." No thumbnail asset is commissioned or created.

## Scope and assumptions

The phone charger, cable organizer, shopping sequence and prices are invented. The numerical illustration uses US dollars for one order. It is not an actual retailer's policy or a claim about average shoppers. Research from a Philippine fashion retailer and a North American grocer retains its original geography and limitations.

The fair comparison holds the charger, retailer and delivery service constant. Only the addition of the organizer and the resulting shipping qualification change. A later household-staple example changes the usefulness and purchase timing of the add-on explicitly.
