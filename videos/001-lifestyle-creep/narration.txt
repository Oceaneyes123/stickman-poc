Sam got a six-hundred-dollar take-home raise. The kind that makes a Monday feel pretty good.

So he moved into a nicer place. That added three hundred dollars a month.

The car upgrade? Another two hundred and twenty. And a few subscriptions came to a hundred.

None of it felt outrageous on its own.

But together, those new expenses came to six hundred and twenty dollars. His raise was only six hundred.

Before the raise, Sam had four hundred dollars left after expenses. Now he had three hundred and eighty.

He was earning more, and keeping less.

Alex got the same raise. He spent two hundred dollars more each month and kept the rest. At the end of the month, he had eight hundred dollars left.

Same raise. Different choices.

That's the sneaky part of lifestyle creep. It usually doesn't show up as one giant mistake. It's a handful of reasonable upgrades that keep charging you every month.
