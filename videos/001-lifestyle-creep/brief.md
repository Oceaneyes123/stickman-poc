# Episode brief

- **Title:** Why Making More Money Doesn't Make You Richer
- **Supplied opening:** “Sam got a $600 raise. Three months later, he was saving less than before.”
- **Target:** Approximately 15 minutes at a natural pace, subject to measured narration. No speed changes to meet the target.
- **Viewer:** A working adult who has received higher pay but has not seen the expected improvement in saving.
- **Question:** How can a raise produce a smaller monthly savings transfer?
- **Promise:** Trace the recurring choices, calculate what remains, and compare two uses of the same raise.
- **Story engine:** Fictional scenario and controlled comparison; one narrator, no character voice casting.
- **Sam's motive:** Better space and transport after years of tolerating inconvenience. Treat him with respect.
- **Alex's tradeoff:** Keep the existing home/car, spend an additional $200, save the rest; show the comforts he postpones.
- **Mechanism:** Sam adds $620 of monthly costs to a $600 take-home raise. Alex adds $200. Existing project math supplies the baseline and steady-state results.
- **Payoff:** Monthly saving depends on recurring commitments and actual retained transfers. Higher income creates capacity; this example does not promise wealth or prove that all upgrades are mistakes.
- **Thumbnail concept, not generated:** Sam between a higher salary notice and a smaller monthly savings transfer; text “MORE PAY. LESS SAVED.” One emotion and one conflict. Use actual $400 → $380 monthly transfers if numbers appear; never imply total savings fell.
- **Production boundary:** Script package only. No TTS, cue JSON, detailed motion, renderer changes, render or publishing. Character appearance follows current project overrides, not the source image's hair.
