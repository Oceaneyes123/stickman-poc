# The Money Trap production rules

## Documentary-first creative direction

- Default: **Premium Editorial Finance Documentary** — faceless finance stories told by natural conversational narration, original editorial motion graphics and appropriate mixed media. Read [branding](references/CHANNEL-BRANDING.md), [scriptwriting](references/SCRIPT_GUIDE.md), [visual style](references/VISUAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md), [teaching](references/TEACHING_STYLE_GUIDE.md) and [media sourcing](references/MEDIA_SOURCING.md) before creating a video. See the current [research playbooks and skill routing](research/README.md).
- Use an [editorial cartoon style](references/VISUAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md#editorial-cartoon-art-direction) for original illustrative scenes and props. Favor strong silhouettes, flat color, selective exaggeration and brief expressive motion that clarifies the action. Match the intensity to the subject and preserve the accuracy and readability of evidence.
- Let the narration and educational goal choose each shot. Use original graphics for hidden mechanisms; relevant licensed footage/photos for context; verified permitted artifacts for evidence. Sam and Alex may be fictional examples without recurring animation. Do not automatically create stickman characters or character-first storyboards.
- One dominant visual idea at a time. Use short, purposeful labels rather than turning each narration sentence into on-screen text. Teach simple causes and ordinary consequences; do not make calculations, formulas or numerical reveals the main teaching method. Preserve qualifications and respectful treatment of financial constraints.
- Each episode chooses its own subject-appropriate visual treatment. No compulsory illustrated room, fixed medium ratio, shot duration, movement quota or template rotation. Use brief, restrained camera moves and clean cuts when they clarify the story; static holds are valid.
- Track factual claims/assumptions in episode sources and asset provenance/permissions in the manual manifest defined by the sourcing guide. Never invent evidence, present a mock screenshot as authentic or claim media is licensed without verification. Label recreations when confusion could arise. Verify relevant commercial-use, attribution and disclosure requirements; a style does not guarantee monetization.

## Technical and production contract

- Engine: Remotion + React + strict TypeScript only. Local rendering, no paid APIs or cloud jobs. Read the official Remotion skills in `.agents/remotion-official/skills/` and version-specific documentation. Match every Remotion package version.
- Preserve user changes and references outside this project. Work in the existing workspace, not a worktree. Flag overlapping existing changes; leave unrelated changes alone. Never commit, push, publish or overwrite another project without an explicit request. Documentation migration does not authorize production, synthesis or rendering.
- Output: 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264/yuv420p MP4. Development previews use 0.5 scale. Motion depends only on frames and declared props. No timers, CSS animation, unseeded randomness, remote render-time assets or additional engines.
- Brand: navy #14243A, white #F8FAFC, yellow #F3BE58, coral #FF6B6B, teal #39B8B6. Yellow = money, coral = costs/loss, teal = alternative. Use locally bundled Inter under SIL OFL. Author original scene vector art; treat third-party media separately under the sourcing guide. Natural footage/photo colors may remain; supporting tones must preserve graphic meanings.
- Design: readable subjects and numbers, at least 96px side margins for essential graphics. No labels crossing faces, cropped important hands/objects, particle clutter, perpetual motion or subtitles by default. Use solid light/dark boards when needed for legibility. Check wrapping, contrast and phone-size readability.
- Animation planning: start from the three motion libraries — `TypographyGallery` (35 text-motion styles in `src/typography/motion.tsx` + `index.tsx`), `ObjectMotionGallery` (20 object animations in `src/objects/motion.tsx`) and `TransitionGallery` (25 joins in `src/transitions/index.tsx`) — and reuse a component before inventing motion. They are a starting point, not a limit: when a beat needs something they do not cover, or a better fit exists, build the better animation instead of forcing a library style. Anything new still follows the engine, brand and design rules above and gets a gallery panel so it can be reviewed.
- Modeled financial calculations live in `src/finance/math.ts`. Derive totals; never hardcode a contradictory total in a graphic. The existing budget examples use fictional monthly take-home USD; label the actual currency, period and assumptions for any new example. Remaining means income minus illustrated expenses, not automatically savings, a complete budget or a prediction. Label debt assumptions separately; do not imply debt unless modeled. External facts/charts retain their own verified sources, scope and qualifications rather than being forced into the fictional budget model.
- Audio: future film narration comes from local VoiceStudio. Keep one global Audio node. Never substitute a paid service, synthesize a fake voice or declare estimated cues aligned. Do not stretch speech to reach a runtime target.
- Timing: edit narration and cue/storyboard JSON, then compile with `src/timeline/compiler.ts` in the film preparation step. Never put narration timestamps in graphics. Use compiled event frames, cue IDs and documented offsets. Both endpoints round with Math.round(ms \* 30 / 1000); duration is rounded once from measured WAV length plus declared tail. Never sum independently rounded durations.
- Regenerated narration or WAV invalidates cue hashes. Manually review phrase timestamps and refresh hashes only after alignment work. The compiler verifies structure, not listening accuracy.
- Before delivery: `npm run check`, inspect gallery and every beat/boundary, render, verify metadata and full decode. Keep technical verification separate from human creative approval and audio listening approval. Record current validation and unverified work in `docs/animation-validation.md`.
- Scaling: reuse existing typography, object motion, transitions, camera mechanisms, props and timeline tools when appropriate. Keep each story's text, cues, storyboard and asset records separate. Do not describe proposed documentary components, shot schemas or license gates as implemented. Document future technical migration instead of altering legacy conventions during documentation work. No speculative platform, asset marketplace or forced-alignment dependency.

## Legacy character compositions only

- The following identity/rig rules apply when maintaining or explicitly selecting legacy character work, not as the default for future documentaries. Preserve `src/characters/`, current galleries, financial components, timeline compiler, render/review scripts, episodes 001/002, research/transcripts, character artwork, voice references and historical QA. Do not delete, rename or regenerate them as migration cleanup; do not rename the repository.
- `src/characters/ReferenceCharacterRig.tsx` follows the user-supplied `references/character-reference.png` with the user's later hair/ear overrides. Both characters remain hairless and earless: Sam in the blue hoodie, Alex in the white shirt and glasses. This supersedes the older tie/stripe look; preserve the separate legacy rig serving shared templates and ComponentGallery. See [character documentation](src/characters/README.md).
- Preserve adult proportions, consistent faces, fixed segment lengths, smooth pose blends and stable feet. Held objects attach to computed hands. Test kinematics after geometry changes. Do not add characters merely to satisfy these checks.
- Episode 002's act-room color script remains its existing treatment (`src/storyboard/BuyNowBackdrop.tsx`, `buyNowLook.ts`). It is not a mandatory backdrop system for later documentaries. Earlier decisions remain historical; the dated documentary decision in [decisions.md](docs/decisions.md) supersedes their channel-wide creative scope.
