# The Money Trap production rules

- Character review candidate: `src/characters/ReferenceCharacterRig.tsx` follows the user-supplied `references/character-reference.png` (blue-hoodie Sam, white-shirt Alex), with hair removed from both at the user's request. Keep both characters hairless. Its palette/identity supersede the original tie/stripe rules within `CharacterStudy`, `BankExample` and `MovementReview`. Preserve the legacy rig used by shared scene templates and ComponentGallery. See `src/characters/README.md`.

- Engine: Remotion + React + strict TypeScript only. Local rendering, no paid APIs or cloud jobs. Read the official Remotion skills in `.agents/remotion-official/skills/` and version-specific documentation. Match every Remotion package version.
- Preserve user changes and the references outside this project. Never commit, push, publish or overwrite another project without an explicit request.
- Output: 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264/yuv420p MP4. Development previews use 0.5 scale. Motion depends only on frames and declared props. No timers, CSS animation, unseeded randomness, remote render-time assets or additional engines.
- Brand: navy #14243A, white #F8FAFC, yellow #F3BE58, coral #FF6B6B, teal #39B8B6. Yellow = money, coral = costs/loss, teal = alternative. Use locally bundled Inter under SIL OFL. All scene vector art is original.
- Characters: both characters are hairless and earless. Sam wears the blue hoodie, Alex the white shirt and glasses; that palette replaces the earlier side-part/tie and teal-stripe rules everywhere. Adult proportions, consistent face, fixed segment lengths, smooth pose blends, stable feet. Held objects attach to computed hands. Test kinematics after changing rig geometry.
- Design: one clear financial reveal at a time, readable silhouettes and numbers, at least 96px side margins. No labels crossing faces, cropped hands, particle clutter, perpetual motion or subtitles by default. Most cuts are clean; camera motion is brief and restrained.
- Animation planning: start from the three motion libraries — `TypographyGallery` (35 text-motion styles in `src/typography/motion.tsx` + `index.tsx`), `ObjectMotionGallery` (20 object animations in `src/objects/motion.tsx`) and `TransitionGallery` (25 joins in `src/transitions/index.tsx`) — and reuse a component before inventing motion. They are a starting point, not a limit: when a beat needs something they do not cover, or a better fit exists, build the better animation instead of forcing a library style. Anything new still follows the engine, brand and design rules above and gets a gallery panel so it can be reviewed.
- Financial truth lives in `src/finance/math.ts`. Derive totals; never hardcode a contradictory total in a graphic. Amounts are fictional monthly take-home USD. Remaining means income minus illustrated expenses, not a complete budget or prediction. Label debt assumptions separately; do not imply a story includes debt unless explicitly modeled.
- Audio: future film narration comes from local VoiceStudio. Keep one global Audio node. Never substitute a paid service, synthesize a fake voice or declare estimated cues aligned. Do not stretch speech to reach a runtime target.
- Timing: edit narration and cue/storyboard JSON, then compile with `src/timeline/compiler.ts` in the film preparation step. Never put narration timestamps in graphics. Use compiled event frames, cue IDs and documented offsets. Both endpoints round with Math.round(ms \* 30 / 1000); duration is rounded once from measured WAV length plus declared tail. Never sum independently rounded durations.
- Regenerated narration or WAV invalidates cue hashes. Manually review phrase timestamps and refresh hashes only after alignment work. The compiler verifies structure, not listening accuracy.
- Before delivery: `npm run check`, inspect gallery and every beat/boundary, render, verify metadata and full decode. Keep technical verification separate from human creative approval and audio listening approval. Record current validation and unverified work in `docs/animation-validation.md`.
- Scaling: compose existing rigs, props and scene templates from data. Keep each story's text, cues and storyboard separate. No speculative platform, asset marketplace or forced-alignment dependency.
