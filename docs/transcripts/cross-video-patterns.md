Keep channel-specific findings separate. Five scripts can reveal a repeated choice; two cannot establish a stable channel formula. Percentages below refer to text position, not source timestamps.

#### 1. Hook formulas and their evidence

**CasuallyFinance: visible event or explanation, then a concealed mechanism or contradiction.** These are related moves rather than an identical first-sentence template:

- “But before we get to the rule, you need to understand why this all matters.” (-X6YzlY_8tM, 4.8%)

- “So what's being reported, and what's actually happening, doesn't quite line up.” (0ll9rEtoRrs, 3.2%)

- “So what changed?” (xtqbRt94TTw, 3.7%)

**howmoneyworks: familiar experience or belief, then challenge it and promise an explanation.** The opening vehicle varies: a question, a film, a market story. Evidence for the recurring challenge:

- “But if nobody can afford to buy a house, then how the f*** do they keep getting more expensive?” (1zjcZ661ups, 1.3%)

- “Instead, I want to try and convince you that what you want instead is a really boring job, a job that doesn't make you or anybody else excited, a job that nobody would dream of.” (4hiVQf9MPzg, 16.3%)

- “But there is one line that still makes me angry seven years later.” (bVM76YxNPjQ, 2.6%)

**jamesjani: weak signal, two scripts.** Both introduce a gap between an initial understanding and a later explanation, but their openings are different. The money script starts “Let this circle represent $1 million.”; the megachurch script starts “I was raised as a Catholic, but I wouldn't really describe myself as religious today.” These do not support one repeated opening formula, nor can this group supply three independent-video quotes.
