# Longform transcript analysis

Evidence-only analysis of the supplied local text. No outside channel knowledge, scriptwriting prescriptions, video viewing, financial fact-checking, or performance data used. Prepared 8 November 2026.

## Scope and measurement

The folder contains multiple channel groups, so “this channel” does not identify a single corpus. This report covers **all 12 unique `.txt` transcripts directly inside `*/longform/transcribed/`**, grouped by channel. `ALL.txt` duplicates, the `short` folder, the other two unclassified folders, and `_raw` media are excluded. A raw recording without a corresponding transcript is not a script sample. Nothing here establishes whether the other unclassified files are longform.

Every source declares: “SOURCE: local speech recognition (faster-whisper small, CPU/int8); may contain errors”. Titles come from each file’s `TITLE:` field. Quotes reproduce the supplied text, including errors, censorship, capitalization and missing punctuation.

**Words:** whitespace-separated tokens in the transcript body; metadata excluded; numerals and standalone punctuation tokens count if separated by spaces. **Runtime:** words ÷ 150; a hypothetical constant-rate reading, not measured video length. **Positions:** words before the exact quoted opening ÷ total body words. Thus percentage of text equals estimated percentage of runtime only under that constant-rate assumption. Sponsor text and inserted speech remain included.

**Sentence proxy:** split after `.`, `!` or `?` when followed by whitespace; protect U.S., U.K., Mr., Dr., Ms. and Mrs. Decimal points remain intact. Other abbreviations and recognition punctuation can still distort the result. Sentence counts are text-delimiter counts, not verified spoken sentences. **Sections:** manually coded major rhetorical units, with nested examples listed when they change the explanation; an interpretive map, not recovered production chapters. Positions use the first exact occurrence of each opening.

An interpretation such as “injustice” identifies what a line invokes, not a measured audience emotion. “Retention device” means a textual invitation to keep listening, not proof that viewers stayed. The supplied scripts’ financial assertions are analyzed as writing; this report does not endorse them.

## Step 3 — A provisional writing blueprint

The following fill-in text is newly constructed scaffolding, **not a quote** and not a proven success formula. Each choice is tied to quoted evidence. Target words are an editorial allocation chosen to fit observed lengths; they are not recovered averages for corresponding beats. Build original wording and examples. The transcripts establish script structures, not a stickman staging system.

### Three hook variations

1. **CF concealed cause:** “[Visible event] is being explained as [popular explanation]. But [counterexample] does not fit. To understand [personal consequence], follow [overlooked mechanism].” Basis: “But that explanation only gets you so far.” (bond market); “It starts in Tokyo.” (Japan); “What's actually happening is much deeper.” (housing/CF). These support the sequence, not the invented fill-in wording.
2. **HMW ordinary-belief reversal:** “If [apparently obvious condition], how can [opposite outcome] keep happening? [Number or group of mechanisms] explains it.” Basis: “But if nobody can afford to buy a house, then how the f*** do they keep getting more expensive?”; “there are three reasons why it could get a lot worse before it gets better.” Alternative entry: a recognizable scene, supported by “I was watching the 1999 movie Office Space the other day”.
3. **JJ scale-to-viewer-to-question — weak signal, one money script:** “[Unit] represents [amount]. Compare it with [larger amount]. Now consider [viewer's situation]. What if [initial belief] misses [explanatory model]?” Basis: “Let this circle represent $1 million.”; “And then there's you.”; “But what if I told you, you were wrong?” This is not supported as a repeated JJ opening pattern.

### Ten data-derived do / don't rules

These are instructions for an adaptation of the observed style, not universal advice or proven performance rules.

1. **Do define a specific puzzle; don't substitute a broad finance topic.** Evidence: “But if nobody can afford to buy a house, then how the f*** do they keep getting more expensive?” (1zjcZ661ups, 1.3%)

2. **Do challenge the initial explanation with an explicit turn; don't claim every script opens with a question.** Evidence: “But that explanation only gets you so far.” (0ll9rEtoRrs, 4.6%)

3. **Do explain incentives through an actor or role when using the CF example style; don't introduce an unexplained mechanism alone.** Evidence: “Let's pretend you own Munder Difflin, the biggest paper mill in town.” (0ll9rEtoRrs, 7.7%)

4. **Do translate jargon into behavior; don't assume naming a term explains it.** Evidence: “That extra is called the spread. It's the market's way of saying how nervous it is about you paying the money back.” (xtqbRt94TTw, 53.9%)

5. **Do use comparisons tied to the claim; don't impose round numbers on every passage.** Evidence: “Tesla, a far more exciting business, paid its median employee just $56,163.” (4hiVQf9MPzg, 58.5%)

6. **Do give a list an explicit organizing principle when using the HMW list style; don't treat every section as an unrelated tip.** Evidence: “But the four parties that make the short-term rental market work, the customers, the hosts, the public, and the platforms are all now dealing with four unique problems that are threatening to undo this market and take out a lot of other real estate investments with it.” (vT6qh72wCZk, 12.7%)

7. **Do let an objection alter or qualify the explanation; don't present the first inference as the whole answer.** Evidence: “There are great managers and there are terrible managers in every kind of company, but you are much more likely to run into one in a job that you thought was going to be exciting.” (4hiVQf9MPzg, 78.8%)

8. **Do return explicitly to a delayed thread; don't assume every planted line in these sources is perfectly resolved.** Evidence: “Airbnb is spending hundreds of millions of dollars across the world fighting legal battles for the right to operate and that's why they still spend so much on advertising their service as a way to see a new city with a local host.” (vT6qh72wCZk, 81.8%)

9. **Do distinguish explanation from final consequence or meaning; don't end the adaptation merely by naming the mechanism.** Evidence: “Paying off the mortgage, buying your dream car, traveling around the world, never having to worry about your financial situation, it's the freedom that you were looking for.” (6mRbDEtDoyA, 74.7%)

10. **Do make the outro match the selected family; don't impose one universal CTA or one-action limit on this corpus.** Evidence: “And if you want to watch these videos a day early and get articles that will never be made into videos for YouTube, then subscribe to my email newsletter Compounded Daily to keep on learning how money works.” (1zjcZ661ups, 98.3%)
